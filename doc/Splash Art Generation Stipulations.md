# Arcanum Veritas Splash Art Generation Stipulations

Use these stipulations when writing Midjourney prompts for initiative splash art. Each character needs an immediately recognizable normal version and a visibly distinct low-health version. Enemies can also receive empowered phase variants. Character identity has highest priority throughout generation.

## Character identity

Use the latest user-approved render as the visual authority, alongside the original character image for details lost during generation. Preserve facial proportions, anatomy, complexion, hair colors, eyes, ears, horns, signature equipment, and costume motifs. A new pose must retain those identifying features.

Describe exact colors and their locations. For mismatched eyes or divided hair colors, specify viewer-left and viewer-right. Compare horn number, roots, direction, and curvature against the character reference. Match weapon silhouette, blade orientation, guard, ornamentation, and grip.

Changes such as altered clothing placement, new magic, or phase transformations should follow the character brief. Record approved changes in the brief before reusing them.

## Shared rendering

The approved chest-up Zariel portrait establishes the roster's general rendering language. Use crisp ink contours, broad sculpted painted shadows, clean readable midtones, and selective texture on hair, engraved metal, scales, and cloth. Keep faces legible and facial transitions soft enough to preserve the character's intended expression and apparent age.

Each character retains their own palette. Inspect a style transfer for borrowed skin colors, rim lighting, hair colors, clothing, or weapon designs. If a style reference repeatedly darkens the result, simplify the references for the color correction pass.

## Lighting and colors

Use soft neutral frontal illumination as the default. Show the actual complexion on the forehead, cheekbones, muzzle, ears, neck, and exposed hands. Reserve the deepest shadows for small occluded areas and deep folds. Keep the character's defining colors readable at initiative size.

White hair should read pearl white or silver-white. Blond sections should read cream, honey, or gold according to the reference. Warm backlighting can tint white hair pink and obscure blue skin, so specify the intended highlight and shadow colors when correcting lighting.

Magic may add local reflected light. Keep that reflection close enough to its source that skin, armor, and cloth retain their established colors. Richard's shadow-covered low-health face is an intentional exception to the default face lighting.

## Composition and pose

Use a vertical 2:3 canvas, expressed as `--ar 2:3`. Prioritize the face, defining gesture, and signature equipment within that frame. Allow blades, spear shafts, hands, cape ends, hair, and limbs to crop when the pose requires it. Keep the body large enough to read in the initiative display.

Treat a substantial pose change as a new composition. Full-body character references can pull the result back toward their original stance. Tight crops of the head, costume, and weapon help supply identity while the pose reference supplies body geometry.

Describe the pose through visible relationships. Specify torso turn, shoulder slope, elbow location, wrist rotation, hand shape, palm direction, and which limbs pass in front of the body. Identify the near hand and its foreshortening. State the location of each hand independently. When limbs touch, name the contact point explicitly.

Use the pose reference as the visual authority for the gesture. Keep the textual description consistent with it. A clenched fist, bracing grip, open palm, and crossed forearm each create a different gesture.

## References and prompting

Use Midjourney and a single copyable prompt as the default workflow. The user prefers direct prompts over masking and multi-step Editor instructions. Offer targeted masking only when requested or when explaining an optional correction.

Assign every reference a clear purpose before the prompt.

| Reference role | Material to supply |
| --- | --- |
| Composition or pose | The chosen pose image, or an image to edit for a small change |
| Character identity | Approved character render and tight crops of defining anatomy or costume |
| Weapon or accessory | A crop showing its complete identifying shape |
| Style | The approved roster style portrait |
| Palette | The approved image with the intended skin, hair, cloth, and metal colors |

Explain which images belong in Attach to prompt, Image Prompt, or Style Reference for the workflow being used. Treat attachment order as an organizational aid. Pose accuracy and identity still require explicit instructions and review.

For a fresh pose, lead with the pose reference and use cropped character references. For a small correction, lead with the current approved composition and add the specific detail reference. Keep correction prompts focused on the requested change and include a short list of the essential features to preserve.

Reference images may transfer lettering, signatures, scenery, hairstyles, costume, rendering, and effects. Prefer clean crops or a pose sketch when these interfere. If lettering appears in generated art, request reconstruction of the underlying backdrop or garment. Aim for a clean character asset with a plain backdrop.

## Normal and low-health variants

The normal portrait establishes the character's healthy appearance and signature gesture. The low-health portrait should communicate its state immediately through a combination of expression, posture, damage, and character-specific changes.

A slight scowl or tiny scratch often disappears at initiative size. Use a few large readable cues, such as a buckled shield, broken rib, torn breastplate, withered arm, split spell ring, or fractured faceplate. Keep damage compatible with the materials and anatomy.

Choose the emotional response for the character. Exhaustion, horror, defiance, exaltation, and dangerous delight can all express low health. New low-health poses are welcome. Preserve face scale and placement closely enough for a readable state change, then test the actual swap.

Keep the normal, low-health, and empowered states visually distinct. A phase transition can introduce stronger magic and a commanding gesture. Low health should visibly affect the character's body, equipment, control, or bearing.

## Frequent failures and corrections

| Visible problem | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Original pose dominates | Start a fresh composition with the pose reference and cropped character details. |
| Wrong second hand or crossed arms | Describe each limb from shoulder to fingertips, including depth and contact. |
| Face and hair become too dark | Specify frontal fill, complexion midtones, and white or blond highlight colors. |
| Character acquires a reference's skin or clothing | Reassert the approved palette and identity with targeted detail references. |
| Eyes become white glowing patches | Specify eye shape, iris color, pupil and restrained glow for that character. |
| Weapon changes into a scythe or generic blade | Supply the original weapon crop and describe the blade, mount, guard, and shaft alignment. |
| Damage barely registers | Enlarge two or three character-specific cues and evaluate at display size. |
| A second mask or face appears in the spirit | Keep the character's single faceplate attached and describe vapor emerging directly through its cracks. |
| Kintsugi becomes molten magical cracks | Specify flush metallic repair seams with reflected highlights and natural skin between them. |
| Red light replaces Rory's spirit magic | Assign cyan-blue to the aura and preserve crimson in her costume and blood. |
| Shoulder ornament appears detached | Integrate the relief into the curved plate and align it with the shoulder's perspective. |

## Reusable prompt structure

Create a [normal, low-health, empowered] initiative splash of [character]. Use [reference] for the exact pose and camera angle. Describe the torso, shoulders, both arms, hands, head direction, and near-versus-far perspective. Place the face and signature gesture prominently within the vertical crop.

Match [approved character reference] for facial proportions, anatomy, complexion, hair, eyes, costume, and weapon. Name the few details most likely to drift. Describe the state-specific expression, damage, or magic with clear physical origins and a readable shape.

Use crisp ink contours, broad painted shadows, and selective texture. Specify the character's intended skin, hair, and cloth midtones under readable frontal lighting. Isolate the character and approved effects against flat white for a transparent cutout. Allow appropriate equipment and trailing elements to extend beyond the frame. --ar 2:3

## Approval and delivery

Review identity, pose, palette, rendering, and state readability separately before calling an asset final. Compare the normal and low-health versions on the same background at the app's actual display size. Check the silhouette, eyes, face proportions, hand anatomy, grip, weapon construction, and material integration.

A white generation backdrop still requires background removal for the final asset. Export transparent PNGs with matching canvas proportions and intentional placement. Preserve pale hair, fine webbing, translucent spirits, and magic edges during cleanup. Remove stray lettering and unwanted ground shadows. Verify alpha transparency and in-app rendering before calling the pair ready for import.

Choose final pixel dimensions and filename conventions to match the app's asset requirements. The established generation ratio is 2:3. Exact export resolution and automation settings require confirmation from the application or its current assets.
